# Research Vietnam fiscal receipt and e-invoice obligations

Type: research
Status: resolved

## Question

Assuming the cafe operates in Vietnam, use current primary government and legal sources to determine which sales records, receipts, or electronic invoices a single cafe must create, retain, transmit, or make available in 2026; when a cash-register-generated e-invoice regime applies; and whether an internal PDF receipt alone can satisfy opening-day obligations. Separate legal facts from choices the owner or accountant must make, and flag the jurisdiction assumption prominently. This research informs planning and is not legal advice.

## Answer

Assuming the seller is in Vietnam and opens on or after 1 July 2026, an internal PDF receipt alone is not a sufficient fiscal-invoice plan. The current framework requires a registered, structured electronic-invoice path with prescribed data, buyer retrieval, retention, and tax-authority coding/transmission as applicable. Food service is expressly within the direct-to-consumer cash-register category, although an entity already registered for ordinary coded/uncoded e-invoices need not register a second cash-register form. A household/individual business is mandatorily in an e-invoice path when annual revenue is over VND 1 billion; a company/economic organization does not get that household revenue exemption.

Wayfinder should make an external fiscal-invoice provider adapter an opening-day boundary, distinct from the PDF receipt renderer, and require durable offline queueing/reconciliation. Legal form, expected revenue, invoice type/provider, exact invoice trigger for pay-before/pay-after cafe flows, correction procedure, and retention period must be confirmed with a Vietnamese accountant/tax professional and the directly managing tax authority. This is planning research, not legal advice.
